# P-011-EEBDS-001 — TETIC Timeline System

**Canonical parent:** `lippytm/Prompt-11-`  
**System:** Time–Eternity–Technology Innovation Continuum  
**Repository role:** Versioned timelines, time-series analysis, simulations, forecasts, checkpoints, corrections, supersession, retirement, and archives  
**Status:** Architecture specification v0.3  

## Boundary

AI-Time-Machines provides practical tools for time-series data, forecasting, versions, event histories, simulations, checkpoints, and recovery.

It does not provide literal physical time travel, proof of quantum immortality, perfect prediction, or certainty about future events. Forecasts are model outputs with assumptions, uncertainty, data limitations, and potential error.

## Purpose

The TETIC Timeline System gives every Encyclopedia Character–Ecosystem Innovation Unit a recoverable history and a responsible future-planning process.

It connects:

1. past evidence;
2. present conditions;
3. future scenarios;
4. Quantum Questions;
5. prototypes;
6. quality checkpoints;
7. delivery events;
8. measured outcomes;
9. evolutionary changes;
10. archival stewardship.

## TETIC lifecycle

### 1. Past Evidence

Capture the history, sources, prior art, failures, incidents, corrections, and lessons relevant to the CEIU.

### 2. Present Conditions

Record what is directly observed now. Separate observations from interpretations and forecasts.

### 3. Future Possibilities

Create multiple scenarios. Do not present one forecast as destiny.

### 4. Quantum Questions

- **IF:** model consequences and evidence requirements;
- **MAYBE:** create alternative branches and uncertainty;
- **WHY NOT:** propose inventions and better systems;
- **DON'T DO THAT:** mark prohibited or unsafe branches.

### 5. Prototype Timeline

Build a bounded pilot in a controlled environment. Define rollback and exit criteria before launch.

### 6. Quality Timeline

Store requirements, test results, defects, risks, reviewers, and gate decisions as versioned checkpoints.

### 7. Delivery Timeline

Record the exact approved version, media, audience, channel, and approval scope.

### 8. Outcome Timeline

Compare expected results with measured results. Preserve negative findings and unexpected effects.

### 9. Evolution Timeline

Create a new branch for material changes. Do not silently overwrite the historical record.

### 10. Archive Timeline

Preserve final files, manifests, hashes, approval records, corrections, retirement decisions, and successor relationships.

## Forecasting rules

Time-series and machine-learning forecasts must include:

- data source and collection period;
- missing-data and quality notes;
- selected model and rationale;
- training and validation method;
- error metrics;
- confidence or uncertainty intervals where available;
- known distribution shifts or drift;
- assumptions;
- prohibited uses;
- human interpretation and decision owner.

A forecast must not be labeled a verified future fact.

## Relativistic scheduling metaphor

In the fictional universes, different worlds and characters may experience time at different rates. In Reality and Build Modes, this maps to:

- time zones;
- asynchronous work;
- delivery deadlines;
- data latency;
- model update frequency;
- customer response time;
- incident-response urgency;
- legal and contractual deadlines;
- retention and expiration periods;
- product life cycles.

The scheduler must use explicit absolute timestamps, time zones, dependencies, and responsible owners.

## Temporal integrity controls

- append-only event history for material decisions;
- content hashes and version identifiers;
- source snapshot dates;
- immutable approval references;
- correction events rather than silent edits;
- branch relationships for major alternatives;
- rollback points for prototypes and deployments;
- expiration for model outputs and approvals;
- supersession and retirement records;
- archive verification.

## Proposed registry relationships

- one CEIU may have multiple timelines;
- one timeline may have multiple scenario branches;
- one branch may produce multiple prototypes;
- one prototype may produce multiple media editions;
- every edition maps to one approved checkpoint;
- every correction maps to all affected versions and channels;
- every retired version maps to an archive and, when available, a successor.

## Privacy and security

- Public timeline records may contain public product identity, version, and correction information.
- Internal records may contain nonpublic operational data.
- Confidential records require access control and limited retention.
- Restricted identity, health, financial, witness, credential, and security data remains in the private authorized vault and must not be replicated into public timelines, blockchains, or model-training datasets.
- Secrets and private keys must never be stored in timeline documents or source control.
