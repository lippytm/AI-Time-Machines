# E6C-CHAR-009-019 — Time Machines Management Systems Components

**Canonical source:** `lippytm/Prompt-11-` / `P-011-E6C-CHAR-001`  
**Character:** Lippy Killjoy — Nexus Nine of the Nineteenfold Continuum  
**Truth label:** `FD — Fictional Dramatization`  
**Purpose:** Translate the character’s fictional time-travel mythology into safe story systems, software metaphors, simulations, and educational Build Mode projects.

## Fiction boundary

Literal time travel, quantum immortality, shape-shifting, consciousness transfer, and multiversal movement are fictional or speculative. Build Mode must not represent them as established science.

In Reality Mode, Time Machines Management Systems map to:

- version control;
- event sourcing;
- immutable audit logs;
- simulations and scenario planning;
- digital twins;
- causal graphs;
- checkpoints and rollback;
- backup, recovery, and succession;
- model and product versioning;
- correction, supersession, retirement, and archives.

## Core components

### 1. ChronoPassport

Authenticates a character, mutation, agent, product, timeline, or universe.

Minimum fields:

- identity and lineage;
- universe and timeline IDs;
- model line;
- version;
- authorized memories and data scope;
- rights and consent status;
- capabilities and limitations;
- expiration and revocation;
- current QA and RiskGate;
- human approver.

### 2. Timeline Ledger

Records:

- starting state;
- significant events;
- decisions and responsible actors;
- evidence and assumptions;
- causal dependencies;
- known contradictions;
- corrections and retcons;
- forks and merges;
- abandoned, quarantined, superseded, and retired branches.

### 3. Relativity Scheduler

Coordinates work occurring at different narrative, computational, organizational, and real-world timescales.

It must distinguish:

- story chronology;
- publication chronology;
- research dates;
- model-generation dates;
- legal or medical date sensitivity;
- product deadlines;
- simulation time;
- archival time.

### 4. Causal Dependency Graph

Maps what must occur before another event or system can exist. Every high-impact intervention identifies:

- prerequisite facts;
- assumptions;
- affected beings and systems;
- reversible and irreversible effects;
- first-, second-, and third-order consequences;
- uncertainty and missing evidence;
- alternative interventions.

### 5. Paradox RiskGate

Risk levels:

- `GREEN` — bounded and reversible;
- `YELLOW` — conditional release or monitored simulation;
- `ORANGE` — sandbox or quarantine only;
- `RED` — stop work and no release.

Red triggers include identity erasure, forced assimilation, irreversible mass intervention, private-data exposure, fabricated history, unrevocable clones, unsafe biological replication, or attempts to bypass HumanApprovalGate.

### 6. Continuity Checkpoint Engine

Creates protected checkpoints before:

- a new mutation;
- a timeline fork;
- a major canon change;
- model-line comparison;
- a merged premium edition;
- NFT minting;
- franchise replication;
- retirement of a character or system.

A checkpoint includes content hashes, source and rights manifests, test results, open defects, approvals, and restoration instructions.

### 7. Mutation Branch Manager

Every mutation receives:

- a parent lineage;
- a distinct purpose;
- a limited capability set;
- independent consent;
- a right to disconnect;
- memory boundaries;
- environmental requirements;
- correction and appeal;
- suspension, revocation, succession, and retirement.

Branching does not prove continuity of consciousness. The system records identity claims without declaring metaphysical certainty.

### 8. Universe Franchise Registry

Registers:

- universe rules;
- physical and technological assumptions;
- cultures, species, and rights frameworks;
- environmental limits;
- authorized characters and mutations;
- products and media;
- franchise operator;
- certification level;
- cross-universe interoperability;
- correction and retirement policy.

### 9. Correction and Retirement Engine

No timeline or product is silently erased. The engine records:

- why a version changed;
- what was inaccurate, unsafe, unlicensed, or obsolete;
- affected products and nodes;
- public correction location;
- successor version;
- archived evidence;
- whether reactivation is permitted.

## Quantum Questions interface

Every timeline intervention produces four reports:

### IF Report

What must be true? What evidence should exist? Who gains power? Who carries risk?

### MAYBE Report

What alternate causes, histories, interpretations, or futures remain possible?

### WHY-NOT Report

What safer, fairer, more regenerative, more accessible alternative can be built?

### DON’T-DO-THAT Report

What must be blocked because it is coercive, deceptive, unsafe, unlawful, ecologically destructive, or irreversible?
